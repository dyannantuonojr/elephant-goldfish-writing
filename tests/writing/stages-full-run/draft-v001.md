# Scope an assessment before deciding on a tool trial

Leadership should sponsor scoping a bounded assessment of document assistance for the 80-person team. The next decision is whether to develop that assessment plan; this report does not request a purchase or authorize pilot spending. A firm-wide rollout would be premature because the supplied record does not establish net benefit, safe deployment or accountable ownership (S3).

## Benefits worth testing

The supplier reports that 20 volunteer legal-office staff drafted 25% faster over two weeks (S2). This is a reason to investigate drafting assistance, rather than a forecast for our team. There was no comparison group, and the demonstration did not measure classification accuracy, review effort, profit or firm-wide outcomes. Volunteer selection and the legal-office setting also limit transfer to an 80-person professional-service team. The full report and underlying data are unavailable.

Faster initial drafting could release capacity, but the useful benefit depends on the effort required to check and correct outputs. An assessment should therefore compare total task time and output quality against existing practice on comparable work. Released hours would remain capacity unless a separately evidenced route to cash savings or additional revenue exists; neither labor valuation nor realized financial benefit is supplied (S1–S2).

## Cost and operational conditions

At the planning assumption of $20 per user per month, licenses for 80 users would total $1,600 monthly, or $19,200 for twelve months (S1). These figures use the supplied dollar symbol; the currency code is unspecified. They exclude unknown taxes, contract terms, training and implementation costs and are not an authenticated supplier quote. A smaller assessment's license cost would depend on its participant count and terms; no evaluation budget is approved (S3).

Costs alone cannot settle whether a trial is feasible. No integration test or deployment owner is established, and records may contain restricted client information (S3). The assessment plan should define permitted test material, access controls, review responsibilities and a way to stop work if information-handling or quality concerns arise. These are proposed safeguards to assess, not an assertion of legal compliance or approval to release client records.

## Ownership and next evidence

Leadership should identify an assessment lead; that role remains unassigned. The proposed lead would develop the scope, cost estimate, information-handling conditions and comparison method before any later request to fund a trial. Timing and success thresholds would be proposed in that plan, not presumed agreed here.

The subsequent decision should consider measured end-to-end time, correction effort, quality, integration feasibility and full costs. Leadership could then decide whether a bounded trial is justified or whether existing practice remains preferable. The present evidence supports scoping that decision, with no procurement commitment or rollout date.
