# Work-document standard

Start with the actual reader, purpose and decision or action. Use the genre that helps that reader: memo, report, proposal, policy, procedure, briefing, business case or correspondence. Do not turn every task into a research paper.

## Decision and support

Lead with the recommendation, finding or request. Explain the problem, relevant facts, alternatives and trade-offs in the detail needed for the decision. Separate current operations from a proposed design. Support company, legal, financial or technical claims with the actual supplied record; label estimates and assumptions. Missing private facts remain missing.

For a business case, expose units, currency, period, recurring/one-time costs, scenario assumptions and calculation basis. Released hours are capacity, not automatic cash savings. An assumed salary, utilization rate or realized benefit is not zero or an established fact. A vendor's account is attributed evidence, not independent causal validation. A polished recommendation cannot establish integration feasibility, procurement authority or stakeholder agreement.

## Execution and audience

Where relevant, specify a named or explicitly unassigned owner, next step, timing, dependencies, risks and success measure. Do not invent names or deadlines. Avoid jargon, internal agent-process narration and unnecessary academic scaffolding. Explain technical terms needed by the audience. Follow the user's style and preserve material nuance.

Policies/procedures need scope, responsibilities, actionable steps, exceptions and escalation where relevant. A gap in legal or compliance support needs qualified source review through the user's authorized route, not a fabricated assurance. External correspondence remains a draft until sending is authorized.

## Readiness

A memo can be ready to request a pilot while the full purchase remains unsupported. State that distinction. Check numbers and cross-references, integrate exhibits with the prose, and quote actual neighboring paragraphs to test flow. Make clear what is known, proposed and still awaiting a decision. Exported Word/PDF layout and accessibility require actual inspection; no reviewer can approve a purchase, sign a policy or certify compliance on the owner's behalf.
