# Wallace and Wray: applied reading and writing

This companion adapts concepts from Mike Wallace and Alison Wray, *Critical Reading and Writing for Postgraduates*. It is not their official instrument or an exhaustive account of the book. Actual assignments and business briefs govern the genre.

## Concept basis

Purpose-led reading produces a short source synopsis: the reader's reason for using the text, the author's purpose, relevant claims, the persuasiveness of their backing, and the text's legitimate contribution. Expand analysis for central sources, including assumptions, concepts, methods and values. The [fourth-edition chapter excerpt](https://fenix.iseg.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/563083097452349/Wallace_Wray_2021_Capt4.pdf), printed pp. 44-49, develops this approach; [SAGE's resources](https://study.sagepub.com/wallaceandwray4e) identify supporting materials. Only actually inspected source content may supply the evaluation.

The [second edition (2011)](https://masteremergencyarchitecture.uic.es/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/Critical_Reading_and_Writing_for_Postgraduates.pdf) connects argument strength to warranting and examines certainty separately from generalization (printed pp. 82-86). It develops comparison around a shared reading purpose rather than a succession of summaries (pp. 54-56), examines values and assumptions (pp. 113-114), and tests each textual component's contribution to the overall argument (p. 195). These concepts guide the following implementation. A qualification alone cannot establish the evidence needed for a claim.

## Companion implementation

These check IDs, report fields and review budgets are our engineering choices, not Wallace and Wray's prescribed scores or formulas. They do not certify academic quality. Apply proportional depth: a short memo does not need dissertation chapters, a literature review or a fixed scholarly source minimum. Work-document use is our adaptation.

| ID | Required reasoning | Accountable reader |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WW-01 | Evaluate sources for the actual question; record the five synopsis dimensions and access limits. | evidence |
| WW-02 | Compare each material claim's certainty and range with its actual backing; expose missing warrants. | evidence |
| WW-03 | Compare relevant sources on a common issue, explaining agreement, tension, method and context differences. | argument |
| WW-04 | Explain each substantive paragraph/block's role and its contribution to the next step; inspect real neighboring prose and exhibits. | argument, reader, comprehension, big-picture |
| WW-05 | Trace the question/decision through reasons and evidence to a conclusion with bounded implications. | argument, reader, comprehension, big-picture, readiness |
| WW-06 | Make the reasoning intelligible to this audience: stable terms, clear assumptions and proportionate explanation. | reader, comprehension, readiness |

## Writer use

Keep a short `writer-notes.md` alongside substantial drafts: question/decision, source synopses, main claim and warrants, comparisons, section/block purposes, conclusion boundaries and unresolved inputs. A synopsis has fields `reading_purpose`, `author_project`, `relevant_claims`, `evaluation`, `permitted_use`, plus source identity, access scope and actual passage locations. Unknown details remain unknown. Do not fill these fields from a title or identifier alone.

Draft from the developing argument, then reread it skeptically. Revise reasoning where needed, not merely connective words. Keep these notes with the persistent writer; fresh readers independently form their judgments from the brief, draft and original sources.

## Reader use

Return assigned checks in `ww_review` using the [review contract](review-contract.md). Each judgment needs actual located passages and a reasoned basis; missing source access is a gap. A single-source or source-free document may make WW-03 inapplicable, with a reason, but missing required evidence cannot be marked inapplicable. Do not infer quality from completed metadata, citation counts, hedge frequency or reviewer agreement. New conclusions must be supported earlier; operational recommendations stay distinct from demonstrated outcomes.
